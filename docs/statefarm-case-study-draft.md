## The idea

> **[CONFIRM] This whole section rests on one assumption.** Looking at the
> expressway banners, the stadium board and the bus shelter together, they seem
> to share a single idea: the message knows where it is. If that was the campaign,
> it is the spine of this case study. If they were three separate briefs I am
> connecting after the fact, tell me and I will restructure around the individual
> pieces instead.

Every media placement already knows something about the person standing in front
of it. They are on a particular road, in a particular stadium, waiting at a
particular corner. Most advertising ignores that and runs the same line
everywhere.

So the line changed to match the place.

**On the expressways.** Save more Jacksons on the Kennedy. Save more Lincolns on
the Eisenhower. Chicago expressways are named after presidents, those presidents
are on the money, and the money is what you save. Three facts that only line up
in one city.

The writing came from the writer I was paired with. Everything you can see is
mine: the imagery, the illustration, and the animation storyboards for how the
banners moved.

**In the stadium.** Now that you are surrounded by ninety thousand neighbours,
ask them if they are saving with State Farm. Neighbour is State Farm's own word,
and a full stadium is the only place it becomes literal.

**At the bus shelter.** As long as you are standing here waiting. Written for
somebody who is, by definition, standing there with nothing to do.

## The app

State Farm also had a mobile product, and it is the piece I would point a product
team at.

It was not an ad. It was a utility: current conditions, hourly and ten day
forecast, driving weather, find an agent near you, call, get a quote, set a
reminder. Insurance sold on the back of something you would open anyway.

transit. One brand, most of its surfaces, all in the same period.

evidence on this site to the kind of work I want more of: a single client, a
single idea, carried across everything it touches.
