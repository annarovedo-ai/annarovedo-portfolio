## 4. About Me

Follow-up chips: **Educational Journey · Working Style · Design Philosophy · Current Focus**

- ✅ Educational Journey — UIUC, Florence, Buenos Aires postgrad work — already in `facts.md`, just needs condensing to chat length.
- ✅ **Origin story — why design, not fine art.**
  Answer: "I thought I'd spend my career in fashion. Living in Italy, I fell in love with the ideas behind it as much as the clothes themselves — the sociology of fashion, how culture, identity, aspiration, and storytelling could all be woven into something people wore. Designers like Alexander McQueen showed me clothing could feel like mythology, theater, and social commentary all at once.

  Working in the industry taught me a different lesson. The runway collections that inspired me were often the creative expression, while the business was sustained by products that reached a much wider audience — handbags, fragrances, cosmetics. Nothing wrong with that, but it helped me understand I wasn't looking for a career built around trends or commercial cycles.

  Around the same time, I learned something that's stayed with me ever since: your greatest passion doesn't always have to become your profession. Turning a hobby into a career can sometimes diminish the joy that drew you to it in the first place.

  Product design gave me everything I loved about fashion — curiosity, storytelling, culture, imagination — but with a different kind of impact. Instead of creating the next season's collection, I could help imagine the next generation of products. Instead of following cultural shifts, I could help shape them."
- ✅ **Working Style (professional-layer-safe).**
  Answer: "I gravitate toward the messy part of design, when the roadmap is still a conversation and the right question hasn't been asked yet. I love the electricity of imagining all the possibilities — exploring broadly before converging, connecting ideas from unexpected places, turning abstract concepts into something people can react to. Once there's something tangible, my favorite part begins: user testing, which is a chance to borrow someone else's perspective for a while. I'm fascinated by watching someone experience a product through their own mental model — where they hesitate, what surprises them, how they interpret something completely differently than I expected. Those moments don't just validate ideas, they reshape them. I enjoy moving back and forth between exploration, prototyping, research, and refinement until the team has something they genuinely believe in and can confidently build."
- ✅ Design Philosophy — "culture is always the brief underneath the brief" — already documented.
- ✅ **Chat-length version of that line.**
  Answer: "It's my shorthand for remembering that products don't exist in isolation. Every interface is shaped by the people, incentives, technology, and culture around it. If you only design the screen, you usually miss the real problem."
- ✅ **Current Focus — specific industries, company stage, or engagement size?**
  Answer: "I'm much more interested in the kind of problem than the industry. I've worked across enterprise software, finance, automotive, fashion, retail, and AI, and I've learned great design challenges exist everywhere. I'm happiest at the beginning of a problem, helping shape direction before the roadmap is fully defined, then staying involved through prototyping, testing, and refinement.

  I've worked everywhere from small agencies to Fortune 100 companies, and I enjoy both. What matters most is working with smart, curious, kind people willing to challenge assumptions and explore new ideas. I enjoy embedded, long-term product work where I can develop a deep understanding of the product, the business, and the people using it — that's where I create the most value. I'm also happy to lead shorter future-vision engagements when a team needs help exploring where a product could go next.

  I don't have a dream industry. I have dream problems — products that genuinely change how people work, learn, create, or make decisions. What excites me most involves emerging technology, complex systems, and opportunities to rethink familiar experiences rather than simply optimize them. Most of my career has been spent helping organizations figure out what a product should become before anyone starts arguing about pixels. That's where I'm happiest, and where I add the most value."
